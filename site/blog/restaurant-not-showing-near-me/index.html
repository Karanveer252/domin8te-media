Why your restaurant isn’t showing up for near me

Someone a few streets away types "brunch near me" and your place is nowhere in the list. Here is how Google decides who shows up, and the things an owner can check this week.

01 · The short answer

Why isn't my restaurant showing up for near me?

Google picks local results mainly on three things: relevance (does your listing clearly match what the person typed), distance (how close you are to them) and prominence (how well known and well reviewed you are, on Google and across the web). A restaurant usually goes missing from near me searches because its Google Business Profile or website gives Google too little to go on, or gives it details that disagree.

The good news is that most of what decides relevance and prominence sits in your hands. The listing, the website and the reviews are all things you can check and fix. The rest of this note walks through the usual causes, roughly in the order we look at them.

02 · Distance and promises

The part nobody can change, and nobody can promise.

Distance is the one part of near me results that no owner, agency or tool can change. If someone searches from the far side of town, a café closer to them will often show up first, however good your listing is.

That also means nobody can honestly promise you a spot at the top. Results shift with where the person is standing, what they typed and what Google knows about them. Anyone who promises a position is guessing, or selling something. What you can do is make sure that when you are close enough and a good fit, Google has every reason to show you.

03 · Primary category

Is your restaurant filed under the right category?

The primary category on your Google Business Profile is one of the strongest signals of what your restaurant is. A Thai restaurant filed as plain "Restaurant", or a bakery filed as "Store", is easy for Google to skip when someone types "pad thai near me" or "bakery near me".

  • Pick the most specific true one"Vietnamese restaurant", "Coffee shop" or "Brunch restaurant" says far more than "Restaurant" or "Food".
  • Add extra categories only if they are trueA café with a proper brunch menu might add "Brunch restaurant". A bakery with tables might add "Café". Do not add categories for things you do once a year.
  • Do not put keywords in your nameCalling the listing "Rosa's Best Pizza Near Me" breaks Google's guidelines and can get the listing suspended. Use the name on your sign.

Our Google Business Profile checklist covers categories and the rest of the listing in more detail.

04 · Hours and menu

Are your hours and menu link telling Google the truth?

Wrong or missing hours and a missing or PDF menu make a restaurant listing look neglected, and a neglected listing is a weaker match. People searching near me often want somewhere open right now, so a listing with no hours, or hours Google does not trust, is easy to pass over.

  • Hours for every dayCheck each day, including the late Friday and the early Sunday close. Blank days look like closed days.
  • Holiday hours, set ahead of timeLong weekends and statutory holidays are when people search hardest and listings are most often wrong. Set them before the day, not after someone finds a locked door.
  • A menu link to a real web pagePoint the menu link at a page on your own site, written in text, with the dish names people actually search for. A PDF is slow on a phone and harder for search engines to read. We wrote more on why PDF menus hurt restaurant SEO.
05 · Matching details

Your name, address and phone, the same everywhere.

Google trusts a restaurant more when its name, address and phone number read the same on the Google Business Profile, the restaurant website and Apple Maps. When the three disagree, Google has to guess which version is right, and a listing it is unsure about is less likely to be shown.

The mismatches are usually small and old. The site still lists the phone number from before you switched providers. Apple Maps has "St." where Google has "Street" and a suite number missing. The website footer still shows last year's hours. Open all three side by side on your phone and read them line by line. Fix the odd one out, and change all of them together next time something moves.

06 · Other common causes

What else keeps a restaurant out of near me results?

Beyond the listing itself, three things often hold a restaurant back in near me searches: reviews, the website and duplicate or unverified listings. Each one is worth a quick look.

  • Few reviews, old reviews, unanswered reviewsReviews feed prominence. A listing with only a handful, or nothing new since last winter, looks quieter than the place down the street. Ask every table fairly, never in exchange for anything, and answer every review, good or bad, in a short human reply.
  • A slow website that never mentions the food or the areaGoogle reads your website to understand what you serve and where. If the site is slow on a phone, hides the menu in a picture and never names your dishes or your neighbourhood, it gives Google little to match. A quick, plain restaurant website that says "wood fired pizza by the old market" in its own words helps the listing.
  • A duplicate listingAn old listing from a previous owner, or one created by mistake, splits your reviews and confuses Google about which one is real. Search your name and address on Google Maps and report duplicates so they can be merged or removed.
  • An unverified or suspended listingA listing you never verified, or one Google has suspended, will not show up the way it should. Verify it first. If Google has suspended it, read our note on a suspended Google Business Profile for restaurants before you change anything.
07 · Keep it fixed

Fix what you can, then keep it fixed.

Getting a restaurant into near me results is mostly upkeep: a specific category, true hours, a real menu page, matching details, answered reviews and a website that talks about your food and your street. None of it is a trick, and all of it can slip again when the menu or the hours change.

Questions owners ask

Can anyone promise my restaurant shows up first for near me?

No. Near me results depend partly on how close the searcher is to your restaurant, and nobody can change that. Anyone who promises a top spot is guessing. What you can do is make your Google Business Profile, website and reviews give Google every reason to show you when you are a good, nearby fit.

Should I add "near me" or my neighbourhood to my business name on Google?

No. Google's guidelines ask for your real business name, as it appears on your sign. Adding keywords such as "near me", a dish or a neighbourhood to the name can get your Google Business Profile suspended. Put the dishes and the neighbourhood in your description and on your website instead.

Why does my restaurant show up when I search its name but not for near me?

A name search tells Google exactly which business you want, so it shows your listing. A near me search asks Google to choose among every nearby place, and it picks the ones that look most relevant and most prominent. A vague category, missing hours, a PDF menu or few recent reviews can leave your restaurant out of that choice.

How long does it take to show up after fixing my Google Business Profile?

There is no set time. Some edits show on the listing quickly, while changes in where your restaurant appears in near me results build up gradually as Google sees the updated details, new reviews and a matching website. Keeping everything accurate and current matters more than any single fix.
