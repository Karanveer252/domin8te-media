Domin8te Try the flow

Client onboarding, step by step.

What a new restaurant client goes through after they say yes. One small question per screen, every answer saved as they go, and anything they can’t answer goes on a list for the kickoff call.

Three ways through

Path 1

Guide me, I’ll do it now

Every chapter, start to finish. Account invites done by the owner with step-by-step help.

Path 2

Start now, finish on a call

The same steps, but each account screen nudges them to tap “Do it on the call”. Good for owners who freeze at logins.

Path 3

Do it all on a call

Only the steps marked Call path: name, contact, best time, then book the call. We fill in the rest with them.

What makes it easy

  • One question per screenTap an answer and it moves on by itself. Number keys work on a laptop.
  • Already filled inName, email and restaurant come from the enquiry form. Nobody types anything twice.
  • Autosave and resumeClose the tab, come back, land on the same step. “Finish later” offers an email link.
  • Skip anythingSkipped and “not sure” answers go on a “To sort on the call” list. Nothing blocks them.
  • Only what appliesBranches hide steps that don’t fit: no website, no domain questions; no Google ads, no Google Ads invite.
  • No passwords, everEach account is connected with the platform’s own manager invite. Our email has a copy button.
  • Why we askEvery question that could feel nosy explains itself in one line.
  • Help on every screenDo this part on the call, email the studio, or book a setup call. Plus five common questions.
  • Owner-sized shortcutsCopy Monday’s hours to the week, snap a photo of the menu, see a sample post in each voice.
  • Time left, not step countThe side rail shows chapters and minutes left, so 50-odd steps never feels like 50.
  • Review before sendingEvery answer on one page with an Edit link that jumps back and returns to the review.
  • Clear next stepsA timeline of what happens next, a calendar file for the call, and a copy of their answers.
