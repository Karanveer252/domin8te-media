
=== bolt.desktopMechanism ===
HOW THE DESKTOP DRAWS ITS LINE (read from index.tpl.html 101-155 and 213-238, site.css 405-440, site.js 454-490 and 535-710).

GEOMETRY. The .scenery svg is 11000x5600 world units (1000 units = 100vh, site.js `unit = vh/1000`), absolutely positioned inside .world, which draw() pans with one translate3d. #boltPath is one hand-written cubic path in world units, defined in <defs>, and the visible line is FIVE <use> "threads" of it in <g class="bolt" id="boltg">: thread--case (black, widest of the opaque ones, lifts the line off bright props), thread--glow (very wide, opacity .03-.24, stroke url(#specglit), the light stop set), thread--body (stroke url(#specg)), thread--hot (#FFDCA8, only under tension) and thread--core (#FFF6E8 hairline). Widths and opacities are calc() of two custom properties set on .scenery: --pw (power, smooth(p,.06,.46), the line thickening as the journey starts) and --ten (tension during the cloud beats). Base widths at --pw=0: case 6px, glow 9px, body 2.6px, core .8px; at full power case 17, glow 45, body 12.6, core 3. Widths are in px inside the world's user space, so they scale with vh.

COLOUR. #specg and #specglit are linearGradients with gradientUnits="userSpaceOnUse" spreadMethod="repeat", x1/y1 -> x2/y2 laid along the first stretch of the bolt (1860,4795 -> 333,5607) so the spectrum (E5322B, F2912F, F3CB3C, 5CB246, 0FA3C2, 2E5FA8, 7A3E97, D51C73 at 0/13/26/40/55/70/85/100 %) cycles along the world; the mark's own sweep is #infg (objectBoundingBox, 4%,0% -> 96%,100%, same eight stops) and #climbg is a vertical 4-stop used by the picker and the story ticks (--climb in site.css is the CSS twin).

DRAWING LAW. .bolt{stroke-dasharray:var(--Lb);stroke-dashoffset:var(--offb)} on the <g>, inherited by the uses. buildMaps() samples #boltPath 520 times (sample(): xs, ys, ls, L via getPointAtLength) and stores Lb = getTotalLength(); atX(map, x) binary-searches the (monotone) xs to return the arc length l and y at a given world x. draw() computes the tip's world x from the TIP keyframe track (track(TIP,p)) plus shake terms, hit = atX(maps.bolt, tipX), then setVar(boltG,'--offb', Lb - hit.l, 1) (quantised to 1 unit so the style only changes when it matters). Progress `shown` is a time-based lerp toward the scroll target in tick(): shown += (target-shown)*(1-(1-.16)^(dt/16.667)), and the loop keeps running while a timed hit rings, so the drawn end never jumps and always moves toward the target. Scrolling back rewinds the line (it is a scrub).

TIP AND BLOOM. #tip is a <circle r=9 fill=#FFF8EC> in the svg, moved with a transform to (tipX, tipY) in world units, r grows with --pw/--ten, opacity .9*--pw. The bloom is NOT an svg filter: <i class="bloom"> is a 30vh radial-gradient disc (rgba(255,250,240,.62) 0%, .3 at 8%, .13 at 30%, 0 at 66%) positioned in stage px with translate3d(tipX*unit, tipY*unit) scale(1 + FEEL.bloom*ten + hit + flash), opacity (.2 + .6pw + .5ten + ...) * boltShow * glow, where glow is put out over the before/after pair and returns for the mark.

THE HANDOFF FROM THE FILM. heroAlpha = 1 - smooth(P, PV-D(17), PV+D(10)) fades the cloche film out while boltShow = smooth(P, PV-D(31), PV-D(5)) fades the bolt group in a moment earlier, so a line is always in the frame while the film's unravel (8.0-9.6 s, leaving top-right) hands over to the page's bolt.

THE RESOLVE. <g class="resolve" transform="translate(9290 760) scale(2.7)"> holds #loop, the mark's figure eight (M100 60 C100 32 78 16 52 16 C26 16 8 36 8 60 C8 84 26 104 52 104 C78 104 100 88 100 60 C100 32 122 16 148 16 C174 16 192 36 192 60 C192 84 174 104 148 104 C122 104 100 88 100 60 Z, stroke url(#infg), no case/glow), centred exactly on the .act--mark prop (--x:9560 = 9290+2.7*100, --y:922 = 760+2.7*60). Ll = loop.getTotalLength(); draw(): resolve opacity = clamp((p-.953)/.012), --offl = Ll*(1-smooth(p,.958,.985)) draws it, strokeWidth = 5 + 20*rvw with rvw = smooth(p,.975,1) thickens it toward the ribbon's 25-unit width, ribbon = smooth(p,.985,.998) crossfades: markAct.opacity = ribbon, loop.opacity = 1-ribbon; wordAct.opacity = smooth(p,.958,.978), rule.transform = scaleX(rvw); the bolt itself fades to 10 % (fade = (1-.9*smooth(p,.965,.998))*boltShow) and the tip follows boltShow. unpinFilm() clears every inline value these set. In story mode all of this is display:none (.scenery,.bloom,.tip,.hero,.strain) and the beats get the 1px x 64px climbing tick .band__in::before instead.

WHAT THE PHONE REUSES: the thread stack (case/glow/body/core as <use>s of one path), the dasharray/dashoffset-on-the-group law driven by a sampled map and a time lerp, userSpaceOnUse repeating spectrum gradients, a radial-gradient element as the bloom, the #loop figure-eight geometry with the same 5->ribbon thickening and the same loop-out/mark-in crossfade, and the existing decoder (filmFrame(t) / requestSeek(t)).

=== bolt.pathMath ===
COORDINATES. Three spaces, all in CSS px, 1 svg user unit = 1 px.
- Page space P: y measured from section.film's top (fy0 = film.getBoundingClientRect().top + scrollY, ~0), x from its left. The page svg (#snk) gets width=W height=H viewBox="0 0 W H" as attributes, W = film.clientWidth, H = film.offsetHeight.
- Frame space F: inside the sticky .hero__frame, (0,0) its top-left; while stuck the frame's page top is scrollY, at rest (after the un-stick) it is frameRestTop = heroTop + PIN*LVH - LVH, with LVH = heroFrame.offsetHeight (100lvh, stable while the URL bar moves) and heroTop = pageTop(.hero) (= the section top). The frame un-sticks at scrollY = s0 = heroTop + (PIN-1)*LVH. So F -> P after the un-stick is y_P = y_F + frameRestTop - fy0; while stuck it is y_P = y_F + scrollY - fy0 (used only for the tip's position).
- u = (scrollY - heroTop)/LVH is the scroll clock in screens.

THE FILM'S BOX (layoutFilm). blit() stretches the whole frame to the canvas box and sizeFilmCanvas() sets the backing store to the box x dpr, so object-fit cannot be used: the box itself must have the encode's aspect. With encode (fw,fh) and the mark's centre (mx,my) as fractions of the encode and the mark's width markW (as a fraction of the encode's width), for a frame bw x bh:
  s = min(MARKW*bw/(markW*fw), .92*bh/fh) * 1.03;  dw = fw*s; dh = fh*s
  pl = bw/2 - dw*mx;  pt = MARKY*bh - dh*my
so the mark's centre lands at M = (bw/2, MARKY*bh) regardless of encode (390x844: s = .443*1.03 = .457, dw 439, dh 493, pl -24, pt 120; the mark is 304 px wide, its centre at (195, 371)). The canvas box is masked top and bottom (14 %) so its edges dissolve into the black ground; the frame's poster <i> uses the same box. Measured mark positions: in the 1920x1080 frame at 7.6 s the ribbon spans x .50-.83, y .36-.66 -> centre (.665,.51), width .33; the phone crop (crop=960:1080:797:0) therefore has it at (.50,.51), width .66, and keeps the whole cloche (x .52-.90 of the frame = 202-931 of the crop) at t=0.

THE FILM CLOCK. t(u) = track(TT,u), TT = [[.45,0],[1.0,4.2],[1.15,5.4],[1.5,6.4],[2.5,7.6]]: the cloche rests while the words are read (u<.45), the lid lifts over .45-1.0 (t .6->4.2 covers steam and the lift; frame 0-18 are static so 0->4.2 reads the same), the mark settles 1.0-1.15, holds 1.15-1.5 while the line is born, and plays out its last held seconds while the frame scrolls away 1.5-2.5. 7.6 s = frame 228 is the last frame before the ribbon's end opens (u-7.9 shows the first gap), so the phone encode is frames 0-228 (229 frames, 29 GOPs of 8). Blur --hb = 6px*(1 - smooth(u,.30,.80)), scrim --hs = 1 - .65*smooth(u,.30,.80). hero.off (visibility hidden + dropFrames) when scrollY > heroTop + PIN*LVH + 40.

THE CURL (frame space, drawn u 1.15 -> 1.5 with an ease-out on the dashoffset). From M=(mx,my) to R0f = (xr, K*LVH) where xr = --rail (gut/2 + 1 = 11 px on 390) and K = .62, with u_ = mx - xr and d = K*LVH - my:
  A  = (xr + .38u_, my + .45d)      c1 = (mx - .22u_, my + .40d)      c2 = (xr + .62u_, my + .10d)
  c3 = A + .7*(A - c2)              c4 = (xr, my + .72d)              R0f = (xr, K*LVH)
  d = M mx my C c1 c2 A C c3 c4 R0f
It leaves the mark down-left, swings back up as it crosses the gutter (the S), and dives into the rail with c4 directly above R0f, so the tangent at R0f is exactly vertical and the page line continues it with no visible seam (round caps overlap). On 390x844: M(195,371), d=152, A(80.9,439.4), c1(154.5,431.8), c2(125.1,386.2), c3(50.0,476.6), c4(11,480.4), R0f(11,523). Lc = getTotalLength() ~ 330 px.

WHY K = .62 MAKES THE SEAM EXACT. The page line's drawn end follows the reading line tipY = scrollY + K*LVH - fy0. Its first point is R0 = (xr, frameRestTop + K*LVH - fy0). At the un-stick moment scrollY = s0 = frameRestTop, so tipY = R0.y: the page line begins to draw at the very instant the frame starts to move, from the very point where the completed curl ends. Before that, tipY < R0.y gives target 0.

THE RAIL (page space). Keypoints: a crest (xr + A) at the top of every .band__in of a non-hero band (getBoundingClientRect: bands are not transformed), a trough (xr - A) at the layout top of every prop .act except the still, the mark and the wordmark (offsetTop chain: .act carries translateY(18px) before .in, so rects would lie), A = min(4, gut*.2) (4 px on a phone so the 2.2 px body stays inside the 20 px gutter; the glow may touch the text edge at .16 opacity). Keep only keypoints with y in (R0.y+60, S0.y-.25LVH), sorted; where two consecutive points are more than .6 LVH apart, insert floor(gap/.6LVH) intermediate points alternating sides so the line wanders on its own through long props; end with (xr, S0.y-.25LVH) and S0 = (xr, E.y - 1.15h) so the last half wave damps onto the rail. Cubics by Catmull-Rom: for consecutive points P(i-1),Pi,P(i+1),P(i+2): c1 = Pi + (P(i+1) - P(i-1))/6, c2 = P(i+1) - (P(i+2) - Pi)/6 (ghost points repeat the ends). Because every keypoint's y is strictly increasing and x stays within xr±A, the rail is monotone in y, which is what atY() needs.

THE ENTRY into the left lobe. The storymark rect R (page: Rl, Rt, w, h; on 390: w = min(56vw,260px) = 218, h = 218*397/720 = 120). From measuring mark-720.webp: the ribbon fills the image edge to edge, the ribbon is 128/720 = .178 w thick, the centreline's left extreme is at (.090w, .50h), the crossing at (.50w, .50h), the right extreme at (.910w, .50h), the lobes' top/bottom centrelines at .161h and .839h. Mapping the #loop symbol (x 8..192, y 16..104) into the rect: P(sx,sy) = (Rl + .090w + (sx-8)*.00445w, Rt + .161h + (sy-16)*.00770h). The entry E = P(8,60) = (Rl + .090w, Rt + .50h), where the loop's tangent is straight down. The rail's last segment: C (xr, E.y-.5h) (E.x, E.y-.6h) E — vertical in, vertical out, an S from the gutter into the lobe's left edge. Ls = #snkPath.getTotalLength() (~6-8k px).

THE LOOP. #loop's d re-based to start at its left extreme heading down, the same eight cubics in a different order: M8 60 C8 84 26 104 52 104 C78 104 100 88 100 60 C100 32 122 16 148 16 C174 16 192 36 192 60 C192 84 174 104 148 104 C122 104 100 88 100 60 C100 32 78 16 52 16 C26 16 8 36 8 60 Z (left lobe's lower half, the crossing, the right lobe clockwise, back through the crossing, the left lobe's upper half). Every control point is mapped with P() so the path is in page px and Ll = getTotalLength() is in px (no transform, so stroke widths and dashes are in px too). It draws over LOOP_RUN = 1.15 mark heights of reading-line travel past E: shownLoop -> lt = clamp((tipY - E.y)/(1.15h),0,1), --off = Ll*(1-shownLoop). The merge: rb = smooth(tipY, E.y+1.15h, E.y+1.15h+.30LVH); la = smooth(rb,0,.55) thickens --lw from 2.4px to .178w (39 px on 390) and fades the rail-coloured body out while the url(#infg) ring fades in (its objectBoundingBox sweep matches the real ribbon's, as the desktop's #loop does); lb = smooth(rb,.45,1) crossfades the ring out and .act--mark in (--rb). At the entry the rail's vertical gradient shows stop .26 (yellow), which is what #infg shows at the left extreme (its diagonal parameter there is .25), so the colour is continuous across the join.

THE COLOUR PHASE. Vertical userSpaceOnUse gradients with y1..y2 = one period Pp, spreadMethod repeat, x1=x2=0 so colour depends on page y only. Two anchors: the mark's centre (markPageY = frameRestTop + my - fy0) must show green (stop .40, the crossing's colour in the film) and E.y must show yellow (stop .26): with span = E.y - markPageY, cycles = max(1, round(span/(1.6LVH) + .14)) and Pp = span/(cycles - .14), y1 = markPageY - .40Pp. The curl's copies get y1c = y1 + fy0 - frameRestTop (the same phase in frame space).

THE DRAWING LAW (per rAF, kicked by scroll). Time-based lerp like tick(): shown += (target - shown)*(1 - .8^(dt/16.667)), snapped when within .25 px, on three quantities (curl progress, rail length, loop fraction); the loop keeps running while any is unsettled or while the tip breathes at the mark. dashoffset is set on the group as a custom property quantised to .5 px (setVar step), so the style is only touched when the value moves. Monotone: target is a single-valued function of tipY (atY on a y-monotone path, clamps at 0 and Ls), so a flick draws forward through every point and a scroll back rewinds smoothly. On a rebuild, shown is re-derived from the new map at the same tipY rather than lerped across the geometry change.

THE TIP. One 72 px radial-gradient <i> (2.2 px core, halo to 36 px), translate3d in page px: at u in [1.0,1.15] it sits on the mark's centre at opacity .6*glow with a slow breath (scale 1 + .25 sin); on the curl while the frame is stuck (page y = curl point y + scrollY - fy0, recomputed each frame; page x = curl x + the frame's left); on the rail at atL(map, shownL); on the loop at atL(loopMap, shownLoop*Ll); then out with (1 - lb). atL/atY are binary searches over the sampled arrays (sample() from the desktop, ~8 px spacing), so no getPointAtLength runs per frame.

TICKS. html.story.snake hides .band__in::before; the snake's crests at each headline's top take over the "line climbing to the beat" meaning. Reduced-motion story keeps the ticks exactly as today.

=== bolt.pitfalls ===
 - blit() draws a full-clip-space quad and sizeFilmCanvas() sets the backing store to the canvas's own box x dpr, so object-fit:cover is useless here: the canvas box must be given the encode's aspect ratio (layoutFilm) or the cloche is stretched. Call layoutFilm() before sizeFilmCanvas() (hook 3) and again on every rebuild.
 - Never put will-change/transform/filter on the page-tall #snk svg: promoting a 390 x 7000 px element at dpr 3 is a 24-megapixel layer. Leave it unpromoted; Chrome only rasterises the visible tiles when --off changes, which is what the desktop's 1100vh x 560vh scenery already relies on.
 - position:sticky dies under any ancestor with overflow other than visible or clip. html/body use overflow-x:clip (fine); main, .film and .stage must stay overflow:visible. overflow:hidden on .hero__frame itself is allowed (it is the sticky element, not an ancestor).
 - The .act props carry transform:translateY(18px) until the IntersectionObserver adds .in, and .act--mark's story reveal does the same: measure props with offsetTop chains, and override .act--mark's transform to none in snake mode before measuring the storymark rect, or the loop lands 18 px low.
 - getTotalLength() returns 0 on a path whose svg was display:none a moment ago (the class flips in the same frame): buildSnake() must bail and retry on the next rAF when any length is 0.
 - iOS viewport units: the sticky frame and the pin use lvh (stable while the URL bar collapses), the first screen's text row uses svh so the buttons sit above the small fold; never dvh (it re-lays out during the scroll). The reading line uses the measured frame height, not innerHeight, so the URL bar's collapse does not move the drawn end.
 - The mode can flip after load (a tablet rotated, a window resized across a gate). disableSnake() must clear every inline value it set (--pl/--pt/--pw/--ph on the frame, --hb and the is-blur/off classes on .hero, --rb on .act--mark, the tip's transform/opacity) and re-run sizeFilmCanvas(), because the desktop's hero__frame is 16:9 again and the canvas box changes. Likewise heroInit is once: a page that loaded as film already holds the 1920x1080 file, so encOf() must pick ENC_16 from FD.mp4.width, not from the mode.
 - The blur belongs to a class (.is-blur) toggled off once --hb reaches 0, not to blur(0px): a zero blur still costs a filter pass on a full-screen WebGL layer on every scroll frame.
 - Keep the mark's on-screen position derived from the encode's own (mx,my): .665/.51 in the 16:9 frame, .50/.51 in the 960-wide crop. The 16:9 file at cover scale would show the mark 495 px wide on a 390 px screen, which is why the box is scaled to MARKW of the screen rather than to cover it, and why the picture dissolves top and bottom under a mask instead of filling the viewport.
 - The film's up-right unravel starts between 7.6 and 7.9 s (u-7.9.png shows the first gap): the phone encode stops at frame 228 and TT never asks past 7.6 s, so the phone never sees the line leave the frame.
 - The contact bar's past-hero flag fires at .85 innerHeight today, which would drop the orange bar over the lifting lid: gate it on the un-stick (SN.s0 + .3 LVH) when snakeOn.
 - The pulses (hero-data.json, #heroPulses) must not start on the phone: startPulses() is already guarded by filmOn, but the hero-data fetch in initHeroOnce() should be skipped when snakeOn to save the request.
 - hero.off must also call dropFrames() once, so the 32 decoded frames (about 50 MB at 960x1080) are released while the reader is down the page; on the way back paintFrame() finds nothing and pumpDecoder() refills around the wanted frame.
 - Stroke-dasharray and dashoffset on the loop are in the path's user space: generate the loop's d in page px (map every control point with P()) instead of a transform attribute, or the dash lengths and --lw would be in symbol units scaled by a non-uniform (kx != ky) factor.
 - A ResizeObserver on .film must not be fed by its own rebuild: #snk is absolutely positioned and sized by attributes, so setting its height never changes .film's box; keep it that way (no in-flow element may be added to .film by the snake).
 - Reduced motion is one of the GATES media queries, so a live change already routes through applyMode(); snakeWanted() returns false and disableSnake() restores the static story, the ticks and the still without a reload.

=== decoder.canvasSizing ===
The phone never sizes the store to the screen. initFilmDecoder line 1023 sets heroFilm to the encode's 720x900 and the phone branch of sizeFilmCanvas keeps it there (viewport 720x900, ts = 1/720,1/900, bc = 0 so the Catmull-Rom path is off), which makes each paint one texture upload plus one quad at 0.65 Mpx and leaves scaling to the compositor. The dpr cap at line 887 (2) and the 3840 cap (891) are therefore not exercised on the phone; on a 3x iPhone the 720 store is shown at up to 1.9x while the text is over it (blurred still on top anyway) and at ~1.24x at the hold once --hz has pulled the frame to .66, which is where crispness matters. The CSS box does the portrait crop: `.hero__frame` is `position:sticky; top:0; height:100svh` inside the 240svh absolute `#hero`, and the canvas (and the fallback <video>) is an object-fit:cover box of the encode's 4:5 aspect: `left:50%;top:50%;width:max(100%,calc(100svh*.8));height:max(100svh,calc(100vw/.8));transform:translate(-50%,-50%);object-fit:cover;object-position:50% 53%` (on 390x844 that is 675x844, showing the middle 58% of the crop while the text is in front, then the whole crop as the frame scales to .66; on 844x390 landscape it is 844x1055 cropped to the middle 37% of the height, which still holds the mark because the mark spans y 35-72% of the crop and object-position centres on 53%). Because the crop is already cloche-centred, object-position is 50% horizontally, so the same rules also render the desktop 16:9 asset acceptably if a rotated tablet ever hands it over before the reload. The frame's scale and the blur/scrim opacities are custom properties on #hero (--hz, --hb, --hs) written by pDraw through setVar, so they are compositor-only changes: no layout, no paint, and getBoundingClientRect is never read on the scroll path (heroTop/pinRange are cached by phoneMeasure). Orientation change: the gates keep a phone in story, resize fires after 140 ms, phoneMeasure re-reads heroTop and pinRange (100svh changes, so the pin's scroll length changes), the store is untouched, and pDraw repaints the current u. 100svh needs iOS 15.4+/Chrome 108+; declare `height:100vh` first as the fallback. Never use 100dvh here (it would resize the frame during the toolbar collapse).

=== mobile.pitfalls (27) ===
 - [both (worst on iOS Safari; Android Chrome and Samsung Internet dynamic toolbars do the same, milder)] Sticky/fixed frame holding the WebGL canvas resizes while the URL bar collapses. iOS grows the layout viewport as the bar hides, so a position:fixed;inset:0 frame changes height mid-scroll, and every step fires resize; the existing handler (site.js:1610 -> sizeFilmCanvas, :887) then sets heroFilm.width, which clears the WebGL backbuffer and shows a black frame until the next paint, repeatedly during the bar animation. Compositing is also worse for fixed than sticky on iOS (a fixed layer over a scrolling document is re-composited each scroll step).
     -> Pin with position:sticky;top:0;height:100vh inside a tall story-only hero band (html.story.pfilm .band--hero{height:240vh}), never position:fixed. 100vh is the largest viewport on both platforms, so the frame's box is constant. Size the canvas backing store from that box once per orientation; in the phone path the resize handler ignores events where innerWidth is unchanged and innerHeight moved by less than 25%. Never touch canvas.width from the scroll handler.
 - [iOS Safari (Android Chrome overscroll glow is harmless, but pull-to-refresh can fire)] Rubber-banding at the top of the page. Pulling down at scrollY 0 exposes the root background above the sticky frame, scrollY goes negative, then a burst of scroll events fires on snap-back; a naive progress map runs backwards, flips filmDir and makes the decoder pre-fetch the wrong GOP.
     -> Clamp progress to [0,1] before anything else and ignore direction changes while y < 0. Set the html/body --bg to the film's ground colour (the black plate) so the exposed strip matches the frame. Do not reach for overscroll-behavior:none on the root (iOS 16+ only, and it kills pull-to-refresh in Chrome).
 - [both] 100vh vs 100svh/100dvh. On both mobile browsers 100vh equals the largest viewport (bar hidden), so a hero text box sized to 100vh is 60-110 px taller than what is visible on first paint: the CTA row / ghost link, and the bottom of any text meant to be 'fully visible', sits under the bottom toolbar. 100dvh fixes that but changes size during the bar animation and re-lays out the text every scroll frame.
     -> The sticky film frame keeps height:100vh (must be constant). The overlaid text box uses min-height:100vh; min-height:100svh (second line ignored by browsers older than Safari 15.4 / Chrome 108 / Samsung Internet 21), anchors its content to the top under --hdr-h, and reserves padding-bottom:calc(var(--bar-h) + env(safe-area-inset-bottom)). Never dvh. The scroll map uses layout units (band offsetTop/offsetHeight and the frame's constant height), not window.innerHeight (site.js measure() :490 uses innerHeight; do not reuse it for the phone map).
 - [both (worst on iOS Safari)] filter:blur() on the live canvas every frame. A full-screen canvas at DPR 3 (390x844 -> 1170x2532) blurred by CSS is a multi-pass Gaussian over 3 M px each time the canvas changes; Chrome does it on the GPU (2-6 ms on a mid-range Mali/Adreno), iOS Safari frequently re-rasterises the layer and halves the frame rate; the blur also bleeds transparent edges into a dark vignette unless the layer is oversized.
     -> No CSS filter on the hero at any time. Before the film is ready: a static pre-blurred poster (hero-still-blur.jpg, about 160 px wide, written by encode.sh; the browser's upscale is the blur, rasterised once, free on scroll). Once the film runs: blur inside the existing WebGL painter (makePainter :813): after texImage2D(frame) call gl.generateMipmap (WebGL2 handles NPOT; on WebGL1 use a 1/8-res FBO pass instead) and sample with an LOD bias in the fragment shader (about level 3.5, 1/12 res), mixed with the level-0 sample by a --blur uniform driven by scroll. The blur-to-sharp reveal is then one draw with no second layer. The legibility scrim over the text is a plain gradient ::after, not backdrop-filter.
 - [both (iOS Safari worst)] backdrop-filter cost over the live canvas. .hdr.is-scrolled (site.css:207-210) applies saturate(1.6) blur(18px) and sits over the frame on the phone; every canvas update forces the compositor to read back and blur the 64 px strip under the header, and on iOS a backdrop-filter over a WebGL canvas inside a sticky ancestor can make the whole canvas layer re-composite every frame and flicker.
     -> Story-only rule while the phone film is armed: html.story.pfilm .hdr.is-scrolled{-webkit-backdrop-filter:none;backdrop-filter:none;background:rgba(0,0,0,.86)}. Desktop (html.film) untouched. Never add backdrop-filter to the hero scrim, the line layer or the contact bar.
 - [both] Scroll events during momentum scrolling. iOS fires scroll during a fling and during the bar animation (up to 60/s) plus bursts on snap-back; Android Chrome may run rAF at 90/120 Hz on high-refresh panels while iOS caps at 60 and Low Power Mode drops to 30. A handler that decodes or paints synchronously janks, and any touch listener with preventDefault blocks scrolling.
     -> The scroll handler only records scrollY and schedules one rAF (the desktop onScroll + kick lerp pattern); all listeners {passive:true}; no touchstart/touchmove listeners at all; the lerp step is dt-normalised so 30/60/120 Hz feel the same; one paint per rAF (paintSoon :1157 already coalesces). Decode pumping happens in the rAF, never in the scroll callback.
 - [both (iOS Safari 15.4+ honours smooth)] scroll-behavior:smooth and anchor links. html{scroll-behavior:smooth} (site.css:72) makes header links and the 'How it works' jump (site.js:1576) glide across the pinned hero, pumping the decoder through every GOP in about half a second (backlog, then a late catch-up jump); on iOS a smooth scrollTo is cancelled by any touch, and iOS < 15.4 ignores the property (instant jump, fine). Anchors land under the fixed header unless scroll-padding-top matches the phone header.
     -> During a programmatic jump set a jumping flag: paint only the nearest already-decoded frame and stop pumping ahead until the scroll settles (scrollend on Chrome 114+, else a 120 ms quiet timer). Keep scroll-padding-top (site.css:71) equal to the phone --hdr-h + 16px. The phone 'how' jump targets the first band after the pinned hero with behavior: reduced() ? 'auto' : 'smooth' as today. Never add scroll-snap to the film section.
 - [both; Samsung Internet older than 22 has no WebCodecs] WebCodecs VideoDecoder availability and the prefer-software hint. VideoDecoder exists in iOS Safari 16.4+, Android Chrome 94+, Samsung Internet 22+ (Chromium 111), Firefox Android 133+; iOS 15-16.3 and Samsung Internet 17-21 have none. Chromium treats hardwareAcceleration:'prefer-software' (site.js ~1010) as software-only, and Android Chrome has no software H.264 path on most builds (H.264 comes from MediaCodec), so configure() throws or isConfigSupported is false and today's code falls straight to the <video> element, the worst path on a phone.

Without VideoDecoder or WebGL do not run the video-element scrub on the phone: keep the static blurred still, still draw the snake line and the mark crossfade (the line does not need the film).
 - [both] Hardware decoder buffer pools and VideoFrame memory. A hardware decoder's output frames are backed by a small fixed pool (MediaCodec typically 8-16 buffers, VideoToolbox IOSurfaces); holding 32 frames open (keepGops = max(4, 32/8) x 8-frame GOPs, site.js ~1018) starves the pool and the decoder silently stops delivering: the flush() promise never resolves and the film freezes. Per-frame footprint is 3.1 MB at 1080p (100 MB for 32), 1.4 MB at 720p, 0.8 MB at 540x960; iOS jetsam kills Safari tabs near 1-1.5 GB, low-RAM Android earlier.
     -> A phone encode at 720 px or less on the long edge (next item). On the phone cache GL textures, not VideoFrames: in the sink do texImage2D(frame) then frame.close() immediately, keep at most 16 textures (2 GOPs) around the scroll position, keepGops = 2. The sink already closes out-of-range and duplicate frames (:1113); keep that.
 - [both] Cover-fitting a 16:9 1080p film into a 9:16 phone at DPR 3. Covering 390x844 scales the 1920x1080 frame to about 4500 device px wide; the Catmull-Rom 16-tap shader then runs over 3 M px per frame, decode stays 1080p, and five sixths of the picture is off screen. A mid-range phone lands at 15-20 ms per frame.
     -> A phone-specific encode from the PNG frames (cloche3d/out): centre-crop to portrait (9:16 or 3:4; check on the contact sheet that the cloche and the mark stay inside the crop through 0-8.0 s), scale to about 608x1080 or 540x960, frames 0-240 only (0-8.0 s, the held mark; the unravel dropped per constraint 4), same x264 recipe (keyint 8, no B-frames, bt709, faststart) but High profile level 3.1 for the widest hardware support, crf 24-26, target 0.8-1.2 MB. Bicubic off on the phone (bc = 0; the picture is blurred or upscaled anyway); canvas DPR cap 1.5 while blurred, 2 at the sharp reveal.
 - [both (iOS Safari for GPU-process suspension and bfcache)] VideoFrame.close discipline across the page lifecycle. Any frame left open leaks a hardware buffer or 3 MB; risky moments: output after FD is nulled (handled), frames after a failed flush(), frames of a GOP that trimGops already dropped, and lifecycle: iOS suspends the GPU process for a backgrounded tab and the decoder errors on resume, a bfcache restore (pageshow persisted) revives the page with a closed decoder, and today any decoder error calls filmFallback() -> permanent <video> path. The slow-decoder heuristic (:1147, average over 90 ms per GOP across groups 3-5) is tuned for desktop software decode; a phone hardware flush round trip is 30-120 ms, so the phone would bounce to <video> after six GOPs.
