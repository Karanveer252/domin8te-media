MONDAY

Monday. Good afternoon, Dani.

You open the dashboard after lunch service. Three things need you: next week’s posts, a disconnected Instagram and the autumn hours. The rest is handled, with the last 30 days at the top, at a glance.

TUESDAY

Tuesday. Approve a week of posts. In one tap.

Next week’s posts are waiting, written and designed: the autumn menu, the Sunday roast, weekend brunch. You read them between deliveries and approve the week in one tap. Nothing goes out until you do.

WEDNESDAY

Wednesday. New hours? Updated everywhere.

Autumn hours start on the first. You check them once, day by day, and confirm. We change them on Google, Apple Maps and your website, so nobody walks up to a locked door.

THURSDAY

Thursday. Know what your marketing earns.

Bookings from the website, calls from Google, direction requests, visits. Every figure comes straight from a connected account and says how fresh it is. Then a summary in plain English tells you what moved.

“Your website brought in 212 bookings in the last 30 days, 41 more than the 30 days before, and the rise was biggest on Fridays and Saturdays. Calls from Google rose to 148.”

Written by the Domin8te assistant, labelled as such and checked against the figures. Demo account, sample figures.

FRIDAY

Friday. Real people. One message away.

A private booking for Saturday night? You type it like a text, in the middle of prep. It goes straight to the people doing the work, and every message is kept on record.

SATURDAY

Saturday. Late night service? Go dark.

Checking in after close, with the lights down low? Switch the dashboard to dark mode. Same dashboard, easier on the eyes at the end of a long night.

SUNDAY

Sunday. Nothing to do? Enjoy it.

Posts approved, hours confirmed, message answered. When nothing needs you, the dashboard says so, and shows what’s planned next. Take the day.

That was the week at Bayleaf. The film shows the same dashboard, start to finish, in under a minute.
