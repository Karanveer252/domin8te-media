Why does Google suspend restaurant listings?

Google suspends or restricts a Google Business Profile when something about it looks like it breaks Google's guidelines. For restaurants, the usual triggers are a business name with extra keywords, an address that is not a real place where you serve guests, duplicate listings, or a burst of edits that looks suspicious. Most suspensions can be appealed.

Google does not usually send a person to look at your dining room. Its checks look for patterns that often belong to fake or spammy listings, and an honest restaurant can trip them by accident. A new name added for the patio season, an old second listing nobody remembers, a change made by a former agency: any of these can be enough.

The good news is that most of the causes are things you control, and the fix is usually to make the listing match the real place again.

Gone from Maps, or locked out of it.

A restaurant Google Business Profile suspension usually shows up in one of two ways: either the listing disappears from Google Maps and search, or it stays visible to diners but you lose the ability to manage it.

The listing disappears

This is the serious one. Someone searches your name or "pizza near me" and you are simply not there. Your reviews, photos and hours go out of sight with it. Walk-ins who rely on Maps to find the door go somewhere else.

The listing stays, but you cannot touch it

Here diners can still see your listing, but your dashboard shows a warning and edits do not go through. You cannot update holiday hours, answer reviews from the profile or post the Tuesday special. It feels milder, but a listing you cannot update slowly drifts out of date.

Google's own wording for each state changes from time to time, so read the notice in your profile carefully. It often hints at what went wrong.

What usually gets a restaurant listing suspended?

Most restaurant suspensions come from a short list of habits: extra words in the name, an address that is not where guests eat, more than one listing for the same place, and edits that look strange from the outside.

  • Keywords stuffed into the name"Joe's Pizza Best Pizza Downtown" is not a name, it is a search phrase. The listing should read exactly as your sign does: "Joe's Pizza".
  • A virtual office or mailbox addressA listing with an address needs a real place where guests can walk in. A rented mailbox, a shared office or a home address you never serve from can get it pulled. A food truck or a delivery-only kitchen has its own rules, so set those up as a service area rather than inventing a storefront.
  • A second listing for the same placeSometimes a past owner or an agency made one, sometimes a supplier did. Two listings at one address look like spam, even when both are innocent.
  • A moved or closed location left upIf you moved down the street and the old listing still sits at the old address, Google sees two places claiming to be you.
  • Edits from many people, or a past agencyA dozen managers, logins from different places and a burst of changes in one afternoon can look like someone hijacking the listing.
  • A website link that points somewhere elseThe link should open your own restaurant website, not a delivery app, a booking platform's general page or a site that belongs to another business.
  • Category hoppingSwitching between "Italian restaurant", "Pizza delivery" and "Bar" every few weeks to chase different searches looks odd. Pick the most specific true category and leave it.

What should I do before changing anything big?

Before changing your restaurant's name, address or category on Google, make one change at a time and keep proof that the change is real. A slow, documented edit is far less likely to set off a suspension than several big ones at once.

  1. One change, then waitChange the name, then let it settle before you touch the address. Do not rename, move and switch categories in the same week.
  2. Take a photo of the signA clear daytime shot of your storefront with the name and street number in view is the most useful proof you can have.
  3. Keep the paperwork handyA utility bill, your business licence or a lease that shows the business name and address. Scan them now, not during a crisis.
  4. Update your website at the same timeWhen the listing and your site say the same thing, both look more believable.
  5. Tidy who has accessRemove old staff and past agencies from the profile, and keep the owner role on an account the business controls.

How do I appeal a suspended listing?

To appeal a suspended Google Business Profile, use the reinstatement or appeal form linked from inside your Business Profile, explain the situation plainly, attach your evidence, and wait for Google's answer. Do not create a new listing in the meantime.

  1. Fix the cause firstIf the name has extra keywords, take them out before you appeal. Asking Google to restore a listing that still breaks the rules rarely helps.
  2. Find the form in your profileSign in with the owner account and look for the notice about the suspension. It links to the appeal. Use that link rather than a form sent to you by someone else.
  3. Send the proofThe photo of your sign, the utility bill or licence, and a short, calm explanation in plain words.
  4. Wait, and do not start overA fresh listing for the same restaurant looks like a duplicate and can make things worse. Leave the old one alone while the appeal is open.

Google decides each appeal itself, and no one outside Google can promise the outcome or how long it takes. Be wary of anyone who says otherwise.

Keep your listing boring and true.

The best way to avoid a Google Business Profile suspension is to keep every detail matching the real restaurant, change things slowly, and check the listing every week.

  • Name exactly as on the signNo dishes, neighbourhoods or slogans.
  • A real address where guests eatOr a service area if you do not have a dining room.
  • One listing per locationSearch for old or duplicate listings and deal with them properly.
  • Few people with accessManagers, not owners, and remove anyone who has moved on.
  • Your own website in the linkKept in step with the listing, word for word.
  • One true category, keptThe most specific one that fits.
  • Proof on fileA sign photo and a bill with the address, saved somewhere you can find them.

Can a suspended Google Business Profile be restored?

Many suspended Google Business Profiles can be restored through Google's appeal process, but the decision is Google's. Fix whatever broke the guidelines first, then appeal from inside your profile with proof such as a photo of your sign and a bill showing your address.

Should I make a new listing if my restaurant's listing is suspended?

No. A second listing for the same restaurant looks like a duplicate, which is one of the things that gets listings suspended in the first place. Appeal the existing listing and wait for an answer.

Can I add words like "best pizza" to my restaurant's name on Google?

No. Your Google Business Profile name should match the name on your sign, menu and receipts. Adding dishes, places or slogans to the name is a common reason restaurant listings get suspended.

What proof does Google want when I appeal?

Google may ask for anything that shows your restaurant is real and at that address. Useful proof includes a clear photo of your storefront and sign, a utility bill, a business licence or a lease showing the business name and address.
