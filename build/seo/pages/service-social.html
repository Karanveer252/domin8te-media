The first job to slip in a busy week.

Restaurant social media is never the urgent thing. The walk-in needs sorting, a cook called in sick, the delivery came short and there are covers to get through tonight. Posting can always wait until tomorrow, and tomorrow looks the same. So the feed goes quiet, sometimes for weeks, and the last thing anyone sees is a photo of a dish you stopped making in the spring.

That is not a discipline problem. It is a kitchen problem. The people who know what is good this week are the same people too busy to write about it. We take that job off the list. You keep cooking, and the posts keep going out, each one tied to what is actually on.

Built from what is on this week.

Every week has something worth posting, even a week when nothing feels new. We start from the specials board and what is new in the window, and build out from there.

  • The specials boardThe dish of the day, the weekend brunch plate, the thing that sells out by lunch.
  • What is new in the windowA new pastry in the case, a new bean on the grinder, a fresh batch of something people keep asking about.
  • The seasonal returnThe soup coming back for winter, the patio opening, the drink that only shows up when it gets cold.
  • Closures and holiday hoursPosted ahead of time, so nobody drives over to a dark room on a holiday Monday.
  • The people behind the counterOnly if you want that. Some owners like their team on the feed, some would rather keep it to the food.

When your hours change, the post goes out alongside the change on your website and Google listing, so the three never disagree.

Your photos, your dish names, your voice.

You do not need a photographer. We work with your own shots, and a quick phone photo of the special taken at the pass is often better than anything staged, because it looks like the real plate on a real Tuesday. Send what you have and we crop, tidy and fit it to the look of your feed.

When there is no photo, we make a designed post instead: the dish name, a line about it and your colours, clean enough to read at scrolling speed. A week without a good picture is still a week with something to say.

Captions that sound like the place

We write captions using the dish names off your menu board. If your regulars call it the Big Breakfast or the Nonna sandwich, so do we. That small thing is most of what separates a neighbourhood café from a chain on the feed. The words stay short and specific: what it is, when it is on, and where to find it.

One look, post after post.

A feed where every post looks different reads like a feed nobody is minding. We set one look for your place, the colours, the type and the way photos are framed, and keep it from post to post. Someone scrolling past should know it is you before they read the name.

We post to Instagram and Facebook, sized properly for each, so nothing gets cut off at the edges and nothing looks stretched.

Approve the week in one tap.

You give us access to your social accounts once at the start. After that, the work comes to you ready to check, inside your private dashboard.

  1. We plan the weekWe look at what is on, what is coming back and anything you have told us, and pick what each post is about.
  2. Next week's posts arriveWritten and designed, in your dashboard, laid out the way they will appear.
  3. You approve or leave a noteApprove the whole week in one tap. If a post needs changing, leave a note and we fix it.
  4. We post through the weekEach post goes out on its day, tied to what is on.

If the special changes on Wednesday, tell us in a message from the dashboard, written like a text. Every message is kept, so nothing gets lost between a phone call and a sticky note by the till.

Posts that point somewhere useful.

Social works best when it is connected to the other three jobs. A post about the new lunch plate links to the menu page on your website, which opens quickly on a phone and shows the menu, hours and directions up top. When a post is doing the job you want, an ad can boost it to more people nearby, with one offer and a few words. When what is on changes, the posts, the ads and the site change with it, so nobody sees an ad for last month's special.

Some places want their regulars to do some of the posting. Freddy's Fanpage is a community site we built for the regulars at Canada's first Freddy's, where people share their own food photos and vote on the menu. A person at the store approves every photo before it goes up.

If your feed has gone quiet, tell us what is slipping and get your plan. It comes back in plain English within one business day: what to fix first, and why.

What if I have no good photos?

That is fine. A quick phone photo of the special is enough, and we tidy it and fit it to the look of your feed. When there is no photo at all, we make a designed post with the dish name and your colours, so there is still something to post that week.

Do I have to approve every post?

You approve the week, not each post one by one. Next week's posts arrive written and designed in your dashboard, and you approve them all in one tap. If something needs changing, a short note is enough and we fix the post before it goes out.

Which platforms do you post to?

We post to Instagram and Facebook for restaurants and cafés, with each post sized for the platform it goes on. The look stays the same across both, so your place is easy to recognise wherever people find it.

Can I still post myself?

Yes. It is your account and you can post whenever you like, whether it is a quick story from the kitchen or a sold out notice. Send us a message if it changes what is on, and we keep the planned posts in step with it.

How fast do I hear back?

When you first get in touch through the form on our homepage, you get a plain English plan back within one business day. Once we are working together, you message us from your private dashboard like a text, and every message is kept so nothing slips between conversations.
