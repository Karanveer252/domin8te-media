Why do most restaurant ads get scrolled past?

Most restaurant ads get scrolled past because they say too much: too many words, several offers at once and a stock photo that could be from anywhere. People stop for one clear reason to visit a real place near them.

Picture someone on the couch at six, thumb moving fast, wondering about dinner. Brunch, a happy hour, catering, gift cards and a new patio all fight for the same small square, and a stock plate looks like nowhere. Our restaurant ads are built around one clear thing, shown well, that someone can decide on before their thumb moves on.

One offer, one ad.

Each restaurant ad should sell one thing: a set lunch for the office crowd, mussel night on Tuesdays, the quiet night you want busier. If you have three things to say, that is three ads, not one crowded one.

Keeping it to one offer does two jobs. It makes the ad easy to read in the moment someone gives it, and it makes the ad easy to judge. When an ad is about mussel night, you know exactly what it was for, and the people who tap already know what they are coming in for.

We keep the words short: the dish or the deal, the day, and where. The menu board name of the dish, not a slogan. If it would not fit on your specials board, it is probably too long for an ad.

What makes a restaurant ad work on a phone?

A restaurant ad works on a phone when it is sized for feeds and stories, readable without zooming and made to look like your actual place, with your own photos instead of stock.

Almost everyone who sees your ad will see it on a phone, mid-scroll, so we design for that screen first. Nothing gets cropped awkwardly, and the text sits where thumbs and buttons will not cover it. The ads use your colours, your room and your plates, even a quick phone shot of the special, and when there is no photo we design the ad around the dish name and your look. Someone who walks in after seeing the ad should recognize the room.

Should restaurant ads run on Facebook, Instagram or Google?

Facebook and Instagram ads reach people nearby who were not thinking about you yet, while Google ads reach people already searching for somewhere to eat. Plenty of places use both, and we tell you plainly where we would start for what you want busier.

Facebook and Instagram both run through Meta. People there are scrolling, and a good picture of Tuesday's mussels gives them an idea for dinner, so these ads are about being seen by the neighbourhood. On Google, people are already typing "lunch near me", "patio open tonight" or the name of a dish, and an ad puts you in front of someone choosing where to go.

Ads work best when the rest of you is in order. Someone who sees an ad often looks you up next, so your Google listing needs the right hours and menu link (that is our restaurant SEO and Google Business Profile work), and your feed should look alive, which is what our restaurant social media service handles. The ads, the posts and the listing all tell the same story about what is on this week.

Your ad account, your ad money.

Restaurant ads from Domin8te Media run on your own Meta and Google ad accounts, paid with your own card, and you own the accounts. You set the budget and can see what is being spent at any time, straight from Meta or Google.

We do not hold your ad money or bundle it into anything. You give us access once at the start, the same way you would for your Google listing and social accounts. After that we make the ads, run them and keep them current. If you ever stop working with us, the accounts, the history and everything in them stay with you.

Kept current, sent somewhere good.

Domin8te Media swaps your ads when what is on changes, so nobody sees an ad for last month's special. Each ad also sends people to a page that loads fast on a phone.

An ad for a dish you no longer make sends people in asking for it. The soup comes back for winter, the patio closes, the set lunch gets a new main, and the ads follow. A tap should land on a menu page or a booking page that opens on two bars of signal, not a PDF menu or a homepage that makes them hunt. If your site is not ready for that, it is something we fix through our restaurant website design work.
