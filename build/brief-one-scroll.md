Project
Build a single-page, cinematic, scroll-driven website for our agency/SaaS studio. This is not a normal multi-section landing page with a nav bar — it should read like a short film that plays as the visitor scrolls. Use whichever site-generation / "10k website" skill you have available for the technical build (structure, performance, deployment scaffolding), but the creative direction below is the spec — follow it closely rather than defaulting to a generic template.
Brand: Domin8te Media. Our mark is a full-spectrum rainbow infinity loop (a ribboned ∞ sweeping through red → orange → yellow → green → teal → blue → purple/magenta, with a dimensional twist/shadow where the ribbon crosses itself), paired with a wordmark where the "a" in "Dominate" is replaced by an "8," under a thin rainbow-gradient rule line. Use "Domin8te Media" as the full name and "Domin8te" alone where a shorter mark is needed.
Who this is for
Domin8te Media is a "done-for-you," AI-powered digital agency built specifically for restaurant and cafe owners. We handle the entire online presence so the owner doesn't have to: website build, ad creatives, social media management, and SEO — one team, one system, powered by AI. This site is our own showcase/client-acquisition page, so it needs to speak directly to a restaurant or cafe owner who is overwhelmed by having to juggle all of this themselves.
The core idea
The chip is the source, not the destination. It's our AI engine — the thing that powers everything we deliver. The bolt of energy doesn't travel toward the chip; it fires out from it. As the visitor scrolls, the camera/narrative follows the bolt on its journey outward from the chip, across the screen, through the noise, and out into finished, tangible deliverables for the restaurant.
Read literally:

* The chip / AI core — our engine. Sits at the very start of the journey, at rest, glowing quietly. This is Domin8te's AI doing the work.
* The bolt — fires out from the chip. It's thin and quick at first, then gains shape, thickness, and its full brand-gradient color as it travels — the scroll follows it, not the other way around.
* The cloud — represents the noise and overwhelm of running a restaurant's online presence solo (a website to maintain, ads to run, social to post, SEO to chase). The bolt cuts straight through it in one clean move, instead of getting lost in it.
* The content/photo panels — the actual deliverables the bolt powers up as it passes: the website, the ad creatives, the social content, the SEO results. Each one lights up as the bolt reaches it, like the AI engine switching each service on.
* The hand — the restaurant or cafe owner, receiving the finished, powerful result. This is the "we handed you your whole online presence, done" beat.

So the emotional arc across the scroll is: the AI engine fires up → travels fast and confident → cuts straight through the overwhelm → powers up every piece of the online presence in turn → delivered into the owner's hands, done.
Visual language

* Background: deep navy / near-black, with a soft radial white/warm glow as the ambient light source — moody, spacious, cinematic. Avoid flat corporate gradients on the background itself; this should feel like deep space or a stage under a single spotlight, so the brand color can pop against it.
* Accent color — the brand gradient: the energy thread is rendered in Domin8te's full-spectrum gradient (red → orange → yellow → green → teal → blue → purple/magenta), the same sweep used in the infinity-loop mark. Against the dark, desaturated scene, this gradient thread is the one vivid, saturated element on screen — treat it the way the earlier gold-thread concept was treated (sparing, earned, glowing), just recolored to the brand spectrum instead of a single gold tone. Early in the scroll the gradient can feel faint and thin (only a hint of color); as the thread gains strength, the full spectrum should read clearly, like the ribbon in the logo.
* The thread/line: the signature element of the site. It should be drawn as an SVG path with a gradient stroke, scroll-linked — thin, faint, and jittery/hand-sketched at the top of the page, then progressively straightens, thickens, and gains a subtle glow as the user scrolls further, as if it's gaining power. Consider a stroke-dashoffset draw-on animation tied to scroll position rather than time, so the line's progress always matches scroll progress.
* Brand tie-in moment: the site should open (after the loading animation, see below) on the dormant chip/AI core — and the loader's infinity ribbon should visually hand off into that opening glow, so the logo doesn't just disappear, it becomes the engine that's about to fire the bolt. Later, at the delivery beat (Scene 6 below), let the now fully-powered gradient bolt curl briefly into the shape of the Domin8te infinity loop again before settling into the wordmark — bookending the journey with the same mark it started with.
* Typography: confident, minimal, cinematic — large single words or short phrases that appear and fade with the scroll (film-title-sequence energy), not paragraphs of marketing copy up front. The wordmark's "8" motif can reappear as a small recurring accent (e.g., in bullet points, section markers) but shouldn't clutter the cinematic sequence itself.
* Motion: everything is scroll-driven (scrubbed, not autoplay-on-load). Think Apple product pages crossed with a moody sci-fi title sequence — deliberate pacing, generous negative space, one idea on screen at a time.

Loading animation
Before the scroll experience begins, show a brief loading screen built around the Domin8te infinity loop:

* The infinity ribbon draws itself on (like the loop being traced in one continuous stroke) and/or gently rotates/pulses in place — infinity is inherently a loop, so lean into that as the natural "loading" motion rather than a generic spinner.
* Keep the rainbow gradient sweeping around the ribbon as it animates, so the brand color story starts on frame one.
* On load complete, don't just cut or fade it away — have the ribbon unwind/collapse down into the single glowing chip/AI-core that opens Scene 1 (see "Brand tie-in moment" above), so the loader visually becomes the engine that powers everything that follows.
* Keep it fast (under ~1.5–2s typical) and skippable/short-circuited if assets are already cached, so it never feels like a blocker.

Scene-by-scene structure (single continuous scroll)

1. Loading → the engine at rest — the infinity-loop loader resolves into the dormant chip/AI core: near-black screen, soft central glow, the chip sitting quietly. Minimal text establishing what this is — the AI engine behind everything Domin8te builds.
2. The bolt fires out — as the user starts scrolling, a thin, quick spark launches from the chip. The camera/scroll follows the bolt outward, not the other way around — the bolt is always ahead, the scroll is chasing it. It gains length, thickness, and its full brand-gradient color as it travels.
3. Cutting through the cloud — a soft white cloud shape enters, representing the overwhelm of juggling a website, ads, social, and SEO alone. The bolt, now visibly bold and fast, cuts straight through it in one clean diagonal move — pair with a short line of copy about cutting through the noise.
4. Powering up the deliverables — the bolt reaches a row of "Content/photo" panels representing the four things we actually deliver: Website, Ad Creatives, Social Media, SEO. As the bolt passes each one in sequence, it lights up/activates — like the AI engine switching each service on, one at a time.
5. Delivery — a hand/arm shape (abstract, not photoreal) receives the now fully-powered, full-color bolt. This is the "your whole online presence, done" payoff — pair it with copy that speaks directly to a restaurant/cafe owner.
6. Brand resolve — the bolt curls briefly into the shape of the Domin8te infinity loop before settling into the wordmark, bookending the loader from the very start of the page.
7. Work showcase — transition into a real showcase of work across the four services, using the same interchangeable "Content ↔ photo" panel pattern established in Scene 4, now populated with actual website shots, ad creatives, social posts, and SEO results.
8. Close / CTA — the glow softens, the Domin8te wordmark and rainbow rule-line sit cleanly on screen, one final confident line of copy, and a single clear call to action aimed at a restaurant/cafe owner (e.g., "let's power your restaurant's online presence" / book a call). No cluttered footer nav — keep it as spare as the opening.

Content panels
The "Content" and "photo" blocks in the storyboard are interchangeable placeholders — build them as a reusable panel component that can hold either text-based content or an image/photo. Use this component twice:

1. In Scene 4, as four panels labeled/representing Website, Ad Creatives, Social Media, SEO — the services the bolt powers up as it passes.
2. In Scene 7, populated with real examples of that same work (site screenshots, ad creative samples, social post mockups, SEO result snapshots), reusing the exact visual rhythm from Scene 4 so the showcase feels like a continuation of the story, not a new template.

Technical requirements

* Single page, no traditional top nav — if wayfinding is needed, use a minimal scroll progress indicator instead.
* Scroll-linked animation (e.g., GSAP ScrollTrigger or an equivalent scrubbed-scroll technique) driving the SVG thread path, the cloud reveal, and section transitions — avoid animations that just autoplay on load regardless of scroll position.
* Fully responsive: the cinematic sequence should degrade gracefully on mobile (simplify the thread animation if needed rather than breaking layout).
* Performance-conscious: this is animation-heavy, so keep assets light (SVG over heavy imagery where possible) and respect `prefers-reduced-motion` — including the loading animation, which should fall back to a simple static or quick fade of the infinity logo for users with reduced-motion preferences rather than the full draw/rotate/unwind sequence.
* Real, on-brand copy — replace any placeholder text with something short and confident that matches the tone above; avoid generic stock marketing language.
